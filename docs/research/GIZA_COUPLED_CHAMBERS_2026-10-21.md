## Complete investigation and decisive question
Question: Do the interconnected spaces of Khufu's Great Pyramid display acoustic relationships beyond what ordinary stone-chamber geometry and conventional wave acoustics predict?
Answer: Currently **NO demonstration** of beyond-conventional behavior. Coupled-room physics predicts possible energy exchange and delayed decay. Isolated chamber spectra and the geometry cannot prove inter-chamber coupling; no adequate publicly verified simultaneous calibrated H_KQ / H_QK transfer data was identified.
Archaeological purpose should not be inferred from physical coincidence. Royal funerary context and acoustical behavior are separate questions.

## External geometry
Approximate reconstructed original base side 230.3–230.6 m, height 146.5–146.7 m. Ideal design: 440 royal cubits base, 280 high; slope atan(280/220) ~51.84 deg. Relation 2*440/280=22/7 approximately pi; slant length/half-base approximately 1.61859 close to phi. Both derive from the SAME 14:11 cross-section, not two independent proofs of intent. Ancient Egyptian seked provides ordinary architectural design mechanism; intentional pi/phi encoding remains debated. Orientation close to cardinal points is established survey context, not evidence of acoustic function.

## Interior dimensional record (approximate; require independent survey for FEM)
King's Chamber rectangular granite cavity: L=10.47 m, W=5.24 m, H=5.85 m. Queen's Chamber limestone cavity: floor axes 5.75 and 5.23 m; roof is gabled (~6.21 m highest), so its vertical modes are not rectangular-room modes. ## Calculated idealized airborne standing waves
At 20 C use sound speed c=343 m/s. Rigid rectangular approximation f_n = (c/2)*sqrt((nx/L)^2+(ny/W)^2+(nz/H)^2).
King's long first c/(2*10.47)=16.38 Hz; short first c/(2*5.24)=32.73 Hz; vertical first c/(2*5.85)=29.32 Hz; King's long second = 32.76 Hz.
Queen's 5.75-axis first =29.83 Hz; Queen's 5.23-axis first =32.79 Hz.
The ~32.73, 32.76, 32.79 Hz triple cluster follows approximate shared architectural module. Numeric proximity alone does not prove acoustic coupling, intentional tuning, splitting or a particular musical key; computed uncertainty from as-built survey dimensions and temperature could exceed the 0.06 Hz separation. The 29–34 Hz band is a useful measurement target, not a scientifically confirmed inter-chamber response.
Published individual King's Chamber field anecdotes: Tom Danley reported dominant low-frequency response near 30 and 90 Hz. Correspondence to ideal 29.32 / ~87.95 Hz harmonics is plausible, not validated through a complete independent dataset.
Chamber geometries can also produce additional modes around 49, 59, 65, 88, and 98 Hz under ideal simplifications; measured Q and loss are needed.

## Acoustic coupling mechanisms, measurements and tests
1. Pressure / volume-velocity transfer through aperture networks and corridors (waveguides) modifies modal eigenvectors and complex frequencies.
2. Impedance mismatch at junctions reflects part of incident energy; frequency-dependent leaks can delay returns.
3. If two sufficiently coupled near-resonant modes interact, combined poles can split; insufficient coupling/high damping can leave a single unresolved peak.
4. A second connected volume can produce double-slope energy decay (early rapid decay followed by slower late decay). This is conventional hall acoustics, not unique to pyramids.
5. 38.73/343 = 0.1129 s one way for Queen's passage (0.2258 s return); 46.68/343=0.1361 s one way for Grand Gallery (0.2722 s return). These are illustrative path travel times, NOT recorded echoes or full inter-chamber route delay. A simplistic isolated closed tube yields spacing about 4.43 / 3.67 Hz; passage openings invalidate using those values as actual measured resonances.
6. Below 100 Hz, wavelength-scale boundary coupling is important. Large passages several wavelengths long cannot be treated as tiny lumped Helmholtz necks. Diffuse ray/energy models alone are insufficient to prove low-frequency transfer.
7. Measurement protocol: obtain permission, synchronized calibrated microphones in King's and Queen's, qualified broadband impulse/sweep source moved between rooms, environmental logging, multiple source/listener points, preservation of raw WAV with SHA256, sensor responses and positional provenance. Calculate H_KK, H_QQ, H_KQ, H_QK, coherence, phase, delayed arrivals, T20/T30/EDC and uncertainty. Compare known-geometry FEM/FDTD model A isolated/B connected/C solid-coupled; avoid extra fitted degrees of freedom. Publish negative matches.
8. Do not claim anomalous physics unless residual survives real site geometry, boundary-loss calibrations and independent replication.

## Non-acoustic evidence kept separate
- ELGabry et al., Scientific Reports 2026: ambient HVSR survey at 37 positions found STRUCTURAL fundamentals roughly 2.0-2.6 Hz (mean ~2.3) vs adjacent soil ~0.6 Hz; distinct from airborne audible resonances.
- Smith and Beibin, EVA London July 2026: comparative chamber impulse-response/LiDAR project; evidence of room-specific investigation, but not a quantified, replicated synchronized inter-chamber transfer matrix.
- ScanPyramids Big Void reported in Nature 2017 above Grand Gallery, >=30 m; North Face Corridor reported 2023 approximately 9 m long and 2 x 2 m; muon data validate voids, NOT their coupling to air network.
- Balezin et al. 2018 numerically modeled radio-frequency electromagnetic multipoles for external wavelengths 200–600 m: not acoustic and not proof of electric generation. Quartz in granite cannot establish generator functionality.
- Khafre and Menkaure are useful potential archaeological controls but lack comparably verified modern acoustic coupling data in this research.
- Granitic and limestone walls create strong reflections but differ in boundary loss; rough subterranean chamber creates scattering. Distinct rooms do not imply intentional musical use.
